# The Colombo Traffic Index

## Understanding the Bigger Picture

Colombo’s roads often feel unpredictable. What if we could turn that chaos into insight? The **Colombo Traffic Index (CTI)** will aim to do just that. It will create a single number that represents how traffic is flowing across the **Colombo Municipal Council (CMC)** area at any given moment.

In this first version, the focus will be on the 37 square kilometre area governed by the **Colombo Municipal Council**. This urban core includes all addresses from **Colombo 1 to Colombo 15**. It is the heart of the capital, not the wider metropolitan region. Future versions could extend this idea to nearby areas such as Dehiwala–Mount Lavinia, Kotte, or Gampaha. The long-term goal is to cover the entire island.

The CTI is not about helping drivers navigate daily traffic or explaining why one junction is jammed. It is about understanding the *bigger picture*. The index will describe how the city moves, when it slows down, and what that means for Colombo’s economy, environment, and way of life.

## How the CTI Will Work

The CTI will represent a number in **kilometres per hour (km/h)**. It is designed to estimate how fast, on average, someone could travel directly between any two points in Colombo.

To calculate this, we will use a measure called **Direct Speed**. This is the straight-line distance between two locations divided by the actual travel duration. The method corrects for situations where drivers take longer but faster routes. It also allows fair comparison across different parts of the city.

From the CTI, we can also calculate a related measure called the **Travel Time Ratio (TTR)**. This will be found by dividing the CTI at a given time by the *optimal CTI*—the fastest direct speed recorded within the past 24 hours. A TTR of **1.0** means traffic is “as good as it can be”. A TTR of **2.0** means that travel is roughly twice as slow as under free-flow conditions. The CTI gives the actual average speed, while the TTR shows how far traffic has slowed from its best state.

The CTI will be built using travel-time data from publicly available or licensed digital mapping services. During the prototype stage, we will most likely use **Google Maps** as the main data source. Other providers may be adopted later, depending on access, cost, and reliability.

The system will measure travel times between seven key locations: Bambalapitiya, Borella, Dematagoda, Fort, Mattakkuliya, Pamankada, and Wellawatte. All are within the 37 sq km CMC boundary. The thirteen routes connecting these points have been chosen to give the best possible coverage of overall traffic conditions. This compact design keeps the network simple and efficient. It allows the CTI to reflect the city’s movement using only a small number of observation points.

## What the CTI Will Be and Will Not Be

The CTI will be a **summary measure of congestion**, not a navigation or routing tool. It will not tell drivers which lane to take or how to reach Town Hall fastest. Instead, it will measure the overall pulse of the Colombo CMC area. The TTR will act as a companion measure, showing how far conditions have deviated from the city’s best achievable traffic flow.

## Why CTI Will Matter

Once established, the CTI will allow **big-picture analysis** that has not been possible before. Researchers and policymakers will be able to ask questions such as:

- Is overall traffic in the CMC area worse during certain months of the year?
- How does congestion correlate with **GDP growth** or **air pollution** levels?
- Do public holidays, weather patterns, or school terms noticeably change congestion levels?
- What is the relationship between the CTI and the TTR over time, and what does it reveal about seasonal or structural congestion patterns?

The CTI and TTR will also help identify practical problems:

- Which times of day cause the most productivity loss for businesses.
- How fuel consumption and emissions rise during peak hours.
- Whether infrastructure investments, such as flyovers or signal timing changes, deliver real improvements.
- How transport costs influence the city’s economic competitiveness.
- The link between traffic, health outcomes, and quality of urban life.

These insights will shape how the city is planned and managed. A consistent measure of congestion, supported by both CTI and TTR, will help Colombo manage mobility with the same precision used to track inflation or unemployment.

## Concluding Thoughts

In the long term, the CTI could help Colombo answer key questions about its growth. How much time and fuel are wasted in traffic? What are the environmental costs of congestion? Are policies making things better?

By turning real-world movement into measurable information, the CTI will transform how we understand traffic. It will present congestion not as random frustration but as a predictable and measurable feature of urban life within the Colombo 1–15 area.
